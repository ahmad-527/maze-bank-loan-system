# Flagship standards evidence review

Started 30 October 2026, Europe/Berlin. User request: deeply review 200 peer-reviewed papers to update the shared flagship project requirements before implementation resumes. This foundation review is separate from the existing per-product requirement of 100 relevant papers and 20 leading comparable apps.

Review questions: What practices provide credible evidence of useful outcomes, trustworthy research, usable and accessible interfaces, dependable engineering, privacy/security, and responsible optional AI? What common assumptions or practices fail? How should those findings become verifiable acceptance criteria rather than aesthetic or marketing claims?

Eligibility: peer-reviewed journal articles or refereed conference papers with verified publication identity and sufficient accessible text to appraise their methods, findings and limits. Include methodological papers, systematic reviews/meta-analyses and primary empirical studies, with explicit study-type and population tags. Exclude preprints without a verified reviewed publication, editorials, protocols without outcome evidence when evaluating effects, vendor marketing, standards documents, duplicated versions, corrections/retractions and unrelated topic padding. Methodological protocols may support reporting standards but are not empirical effectiveness evidence. Standards are maintained in a separate normative register.

Search sources: publisher/author-institution pages, Crossref publication metadata, Europe PMC open-access full texts and refereed-conference archives. Crossref/PubMed indexing alone is not proof of peer review or quality. Search retrieval and citation counts do not count as completed review.

Target breadth: research/measurement; user-centred product evaluation; visual and interaction design; accessibility/inclusion; engagement and behavior; privacy/security/trust; software verification/reliability; and optional AI evaluation/oversight. Preserve contradictory, null and adverse results. Avoid allowing easily accessible health-app papers to silently stand in for evidence about finance or all software.

For each counted review record: stable identifier, title/authors/year/venue, publication verification, accessible source and content hash, design/context/sample, actual findings, limitations/bias/applicability, requirement implications, appraisal depth and review status. Extracted text is a reading aid, not an automatic critical appraisal. A paper may inform multiple requirements but counts once. A meta-analysis counts once; its references do not become separately reviewed papers.

Synthesis: distinguish empirical support, methodological rationale, normative requirements and proposed engineering acceptance targets. Record inference and transfer limits. Numerical targets require appropriate justification and project-specific calibration. Reading 200 papers is a minimum process requirement, not proof of demand or universal superiority.

Completion: 200 eligible unique papers actually appraised; a traceable synthesis and requirement-to-evidence mapping; counts and exclusions reconciled; the main standard updated with specific deliverables, gates, tests and stop conditions. No flagship implementation or claim of research completion before these conditions are met. No interviews or external outreach without user instruction.
