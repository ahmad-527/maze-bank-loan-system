# Maze Bank product-domain critical appraisals

30 October 2026. 118 focused full-text appraisals; 100 retained in the product evidence set, with publication identity corroborated and primary venue review policies mapped. Eighteen additional records are excluded from the minimum. This uses policy-supported inference where no article-specific statement is present; confidential reviewer reports and historical compliance were not audited. The full product research gate remains open for app benchmarking and validation.

This is purposive, predominantly open-access research, not an exhaustive systematic review or proof of demand/effectiveness. Reading covered relevant full-text methods/design, findings and limitations/discussion passages, not every paragraph/supplement or raw-data replication. Context/observational studies cannot establish that the proposed app works. Related cohorts/reviews are clustered. Shared foundation papers have fresh product-specific decisions and count once within this corpus.

## M0004 — Short-term lending: Payday loans as risk factors for anxiety, inflammation and poor health.

Sweet E, Kuzawa CW, McDade TW. (2018). *SSM - population health*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssmph.2018.05.009); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6005810/).

**Counted in product set:** yes; review basis: venue_policy_supported_inference
**Design/context:** Cross-sectional quantitative/biomarker study. 286 Boston adults; current short-term borrowers were a small subset.
**Finding:** Short-term-loan history associated with several poorer health measures.
**Limit/transfer:** Selection, reverse causation and lifetime-versus-current borrowing prevent causal inference; US payday lending is not the German creditor mix.
**Product decision:** Retain creditor/claim types and historical source context; do not imply that organising debt improves health or promote new credit.
**Evidence cluster:** Boston debt-health cohort.

## M0005 — Over-indebtedness and its association with sleep and sleep medication use.

Warth J, Puth MT, Tillmann J, Porz J, Zier U, Weckbecker K, Münster E. (2019). *BMC public health*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-019-7231-1); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6637586/).

**Counted in product set:** yes; review basis: venue_policy_supported_inference
**Design/context:** Cross-sectional linked-survey comparison. 538 eligible over-indebted respondents and 7447 comparison respondents; German NRW debt-advice recruitment in 2017.
**Finding:** Over-indebtedness associated with sleep problems and medication use.
**Limit/transfer:** Counselling repeat-users, language/response selection and differing survey samples; no causal app or sleep benefit.
**Product decision:** Offer resumable low-burden sessions; do not collect sleep/medication data or use it for user risk scores.
**Evidence cluster:** NRW OID-2017/DEGS1.

## M0006 — Over-indebtedness and its association with the prevalence of back pain.

**Counted in product set:** no: Secondary context retained, but excluded from the product minimum because the specific health, crime, insolvency or investment outcome is too remote from the preparation task.
**Design/context:** Cross-sectional comparative survey. 949 counselling clients compared with 8318 German population respondents.
**Finding:** Large difference in reported back-pain prevalence remained after measured adjustment.

## M0012 — On the Relation Between Over-Indebtedness and Well-Being: An Analysis of the Mechanisms Influencing Health, Sleep, Life Satisfaction, and Emotional Well-Being.

Ferreira MB, de Almeida F, Soro JC, Herter MM, Pinto DC, Silva CS. (2021). *Frontiers in psychology*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.591875); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8116657/).

**Counted in product set:** yes; review basis: article_statement
**Design/context:** Cross-sectional questionnaire with mediation models. 365 Portuguese consumers: 236 over-indebted DECO advice users and 129 comparison consumers.
**Finding:** Lower perceived control and financial anxiety accompanied poorer reported well-being.
**Limit/transfer:** Selected advice users, cross-sectional mediation and single-item financial well-being; causal direction not established.

**Evidence cluster:** Portuguese DECO survey.

## M0013 — Trajectories of unsecured debt and health at midlife.

Frech A, Houle J, Tumin D. (2021). *SSM - population health*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssmph.2021.100846); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8219895/).

**Counted in product set:** no: Secondary context retained, but excluded from the product minimum because the specific health, crime, insolvency or investment outcome is too remote from the preparation task.
**Design/context:** Longitudinal observational trajectory study. 7850 US NLSY79 respondents; unsecured debt ages 28-40 and health at 50.
**Finding:** Some growing/sustained unsecured-debt trajectories associated with later pain/disability.
**Limit/transfer:** About 20 percent attrition by 50, historical debt period ending before Great Recession, residual confounding.

**Evidence cluster:** NLSY79 secondary analyses.

## M0015 — Cost-related medication nonadherence among over-indebted individuals enrolled in statutory health insurance in Germany: a cross-sectional population study.

Warth J, Puth MT, Tillmann J, Beckmann N, Porz J, Zier U, Weckbecker K, Weltermann B, Münster E. (2019). *BMC health services research*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4710-0); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880370/).

**Counted in product set:** yes; review basis: venue_policy_supported_inference
**Design/context:** Cross-sectional advice-client survey. 604 eligible insured German NRW clients; underlying survey 699 of 1393 invited.
**Finding:** About one-third reported cost-related medication nonadherence.
**Limit/transfer:** Self-report, service-user/language selection and exclusions; same OID survey as several related papers, not independent replication.

**Evidence cluster:** NRW OID-2017.

**Counted in product set:** yes; review basis: venue_policy_supported_inference
